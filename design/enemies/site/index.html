How they are made

Every frame is computed. Nothing is drawn by hand: each one is a few shapes moved by a formula, so its 8 frames flow like the player's.
The boss is made of parts. Shell, garden, head, neck and four legs move against each other: the shell sinks on each footfall, the head follows, the tree sways a frame late. That lag is what makes something read as heavy.
Big, not busy. Old Mossback is about four players wide, in the same flat colours and darker-tone outlines as the cow, so it belongs to the same world.
Three attacks, three warnings. Each has its own held pose (risen on its legs, head pulled in, shell shuddering), so the answer can be read before the hit: back off, sidestep, deal with the sporecaps.
A first boss teaches. It is slow, every warning is long (about half a second), and it has one soft spot, the stretched neck, that rewards waiting.
It has a brood. Its shake drops three sporelings, small sporecaps with their own landing; in the strip above they fall, pop up, waddle off and puff. Sporecaps, hoppers and burrows stay ordinary, rare creatures.
Off this page. The owl and the stone golem are dropped. The tusker, pincer, strawman, wisp and wyrm are still in the game.
