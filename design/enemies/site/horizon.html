NEXEL WORLDS

A sky over the world: the ground curves away to a horizon, and the land and everything on it stand. Made from real frames of the game; nothing here is in the game yet. (Page 12.)

Try it

Seven places, in the curved far view

What this shows

  • Where you are, nothing changes. The lower part of the screen is not bent: it is today's picture, tile for tile. In a dug room the hole is cut open around you as now; in a house its upper floor and roof are cut away as now. Digging and building happen there. The curve only touches what is far.
  • What stands, stands. Only the ground bends. Trees, rocks, animals and people are put back on it upright and smaller with distance; a house is put back piece by piece (each wall, floor and roof cell at its own tile), so it stays whole.
  • The land is in levels and solid. The world knows the height of every tile. Each spot of the ground is raised by its height on the curve, with earth under it down to the land below, and its cliff is the game's own painted wall.
  • Over the horizon and down. The world goes on round: past the horizon a thing sinks behind the ground, its foot first, then the rest. High land the same.
  • Taller blocks. A level is 8 pixels high in the game today (half a tile). Two places are shown again at 14, drawn by the game itself with that one number changed. It would change the whole game's look, not only the curve.
  • The sky is the hour's: sun or moon on its way across, clouds, stars. The two rows of far hills are painted, not the world's own.
  • Short or far. Short view holds about 1.6 times the bent band's height in land; far view about 3.8 times, which is about 20 more tiles to draw.
  • Not right yet. A tree that stands behind a hill is drawn over it (the page puts everything that stands on top of the land). Land past the horizon is one pale colour. A pit far away shows its back wall and little of its floor. Aiming with the mouse in the bent band, and names over heads there, are not tried.
