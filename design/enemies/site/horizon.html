NEXEL WORLDS

A sky over the world: the ground curves away to a horizon, and the land and everything on it stand. Made from real frames of the game; nothing here is in the game yet. (Page 10.)

Try it

Seven places, in the curved far view

Buildings and under ground: what the test shows

  • Where you are, nothing changes. The lower part of the screen is not bent: it is today's picture, tile for tile. Standing in a dug room, the hole is cut open around you exactly as now; standing in a house, its upper floor and roof are cut away exactly as now. Digging, building, the build cursor, stairs and ladders all happen there. So the curve does not fight the building system: it only touches what is far.
  • Far houses stand. A house is already drawn piece by piece (each wall, each floor and roof cell, each ladder), so each piece is put back on the bent ground at its own tile, a floor as high over the ground as it is built. Two-storey houses come out whole and stand up against the sky. (My first try made far pieces narrower: a house came apart in strips. On a log far things keep their width and only lean away.)
  • The land stands, level by level. The world is in three dimensions already: it knows the height of every tile, and the game already draws each level a step higher than the one under it. So here each level is its own sheet on the curve: bent with the ground, then raised by its own height; and where a level ends over lower land, its wall is put back upright like a house's. The hill with the cave now stands over the horizon, mouth and all; the mountain rises in terraces and takes the sky, as a mountain in front of you does. Untick "Land in levels" to see it pressed flat as before.
  • The land is solid. The first try at levels left sky under far terraces and filler colour where the picture ran out. Now, for every pixel, the page asks the height map which spot of the ground would be seen there at each height, from the top down, and whether the land there is that high: its top if so, its side if it is higher. No gaps: a far terrace has earth under it, a far hill is a whole block.
  • Taller blocks. A level is 8 pixels high in the game today (half a tile). Two of the places are shown again at 14: the game draws them itself with that one number changed, walls and all. Near you a cliff is nearly a tile high per level and reads as a real step; far off the mountain keeps its shape instead of sinking into the ground. It is a change to the whole game's look, not only to the curve: stairs, ladders, jumps and what hides you would all be drawn taller.
  • What is still rough. The side of far land is the game's painted wall with its bands run together (as painted, a few pixels tall, it was stripes). A tree that stands behind a hill is drawn over it (the page puts everything that stands on top of the land; the game would sort them with the land). A pit far away shows its back wall and little of its floor.
  • The build cursor far away. Planning a wall up in the bent band means aiming through the bend. The cursor can be kept to the flat part, or the bend undone for it.
  • A deep place far away. Scroll the walk by hand in "Under ground": the dug room seen from far is squashed while you stand tall in it. In the game you are never far from yourself, but another player's pit is: it reads as a dark slot.

What changed, and what each costs

  • Things stand. Before, the whole picture was bent, so a tree far away was pressed flat into the grass. Now only the ground bends: trees, rocks, flowers, animals and people are put back on it upright, smaller the further they are, and the ones at the edge stand up against the sky. Untick "Things stand" to see the old way. In the game this means drawing the ground and what stands on it separately, which the shadows already do.
  • Mountains behind. Two rows of far hills past the horizon, in the hour's colours. They are painted, not the world's own: a real peak you can walk to would have to be worked out from the map.
  • Curved: the lower screen is today's picture, tile for tile. Short view shows about 1.6 times the bent band's height in land, far view about 3.8 times (about 20 more tiles to draw).
  • Tilted camera (instead of the sky strip): the ground leans back like a table seen from a chair: far land is shorter and also narrower, lines run to a point. It reads as depth more than the curve does, but nearly the whole screen is resampled, so the land's pixels are no longer square except right at your feet, and the far land needs to be drawn wider as well as further.
  • Backdrop (the sky strip, redone): nothing bends at all. The land ends behind a line of woods, with hills and mountains in rows behind it. Every pixel stays as today and the mouse aims as today. It costs about 4 tiles of view, and the world still stops on a line: only now something stands on that line.
  • Still not shown: the world's own high ground (cliffs, mountains) under a bend; aiming with the mouse inside the bent band; names and speech over bent ground.
