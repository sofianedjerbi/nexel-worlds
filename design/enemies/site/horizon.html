NEXEL WORLDS

A sky over the world: two ways to show one, each with a short and a far view. Made from a real frame of the game (a strip of meadow, 54 tiles from south to north); nothing here is in the game.

Try it

The four, side by side

What each one costs

  • Curved (the world rolls away from you, as on a log): the lower part of the screen is exactly today's picture; above "Bend starts at" the land is drawn shorter and shorter up to the horizon. Short view: the bent band shows about 1.6 times its own height of land. Far view: about 3.5 times. So far means more land to draw north of you (about 20 more tiles at this size), and things near the horizon are squashed flat: a tree there is a few pixels tall.
  • Sky strip (the land just ends in haze): nothing is bent, every tile keeps its shape, the mouse aims as today. But the land stops on a straight line, and what the sky takes is taken from your view: short view loses about 8 tiles of the north, far view about 3.
  • Both: pointing with the mouse in the bent band needs the same bend undone (curved only); what is written over heads there must follow; the sky is the hour's (sun or moon on its way across, clouds, stars at night).
  • Not shown here: mountains. High ground is drawn up the screen, so a hill near the horizon would stand into the sky. That may look right (a peak against the sky) or not: to be seen in the game itself.
