NEXEL WORLDS

A sky over the world: the ground curves away to a horizon, and the land and everything on it stand. Made from real frames of the game; nothing here is in the game yet. (Page 13.)

Try it

Seven places, in the curved far view

What this shows

  • It is drawn from shapes now. The earlier pages took the game's finished picture and worked out, pixel by pixel, what a bent world would show: every fix left something else wrong at the horizon. This page builds each place as geometry and lets the graphics card bend it: every tile's top at its own height, every cliff an upright wall, every tree, animal and house piece an upright card. The game's own painting is on all of it.
  • What that gives without a rule for it. Land in front hides what is behind it. A far terrace is as small as it should be, and smoothed the way any far thing is. Past the horizon the world goes on round and down, and things sink behind the ground because the ground in front of them is nearer.
  • Where you are, nothing changes. The lower part of the screen is not bent: it is today's picture, tile for tile. In a dug room the hole is cut open around you as now; in a house its upper floor and roof are cut away as now.
  • Taller blocks. A level is 8 pixels high in the game today (half a tile). Two places are shown again at 14, drawn by the game itself with that one number changed. It would change the whole game's look, not only the curve.
  • The sky is the hour's: sun or moon on its way across, clouds, stars. The two rows of far hills are painted, not the world's own.
  • Short or far. Short view holds about 1.6 times the bent band's height in land; far view about 3.8 times, which is about 20 more tiles to draw.
  • Not right yet. The sides of a cliff that face east or west have no wall (the game never paints one, seen from above). A tile whose top the game's picture hides behind higher land wears that land's painting. Someone in a far pit is hidden by its rim. Aiming with the mouse in the bent band, and names over heads there, are not tried.
