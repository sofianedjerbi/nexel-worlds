NEXEL WORLDS

The land as blocks. Every cell of the world that is solid is a block with a top and a front, drawn from the world's own cells (not from a picture of it); what stands is flat and faces you. A try on this computer; nothing here is in the game yet. (Blocks, page 1.)

Walk in it

Arrows or WASD, or drag on the picture. You go up or down a block at a step, fall into holes, and walk into caves where they are open.

What this is

  • The world's own cells. The game keeps the land as columns of cells, solid or empty: a hill is cells, a cave is empty cells under solid ones, a dug room is a column cut low. This page takes those columns as they are and makes a block's top wherever a solid cell has an empty one over it, and its front wherever the cell south of it is empty.
  • Its own paint. A top is the game's painting of a plain tile of that ground at that height, a front is the wall colours of its level. Nothing is cut out of a finished picture, so there is no rim, corner or cut-away to undo.
  • Seeing through. Where blocks stand between you and the eye (a cave's roof, a room's rim, a hill you are behind), those higher than your feet are not drawn in a round window on you. One rule for every case.
  • Not blocks: trees, animals, you and what is built (walls, floors, roofs of a house) are still flat pictures standing at their place.
  • Not there: a block's east, west and north faces. The eye looks straight north with no perspective: an upright east or west face has no width.
