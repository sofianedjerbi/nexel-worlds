NEXEL WORLDS

Five candidates for the music of the first screen. Cute and fast, each with a theme of its own. Each one loops with no gap, as it would behind the menu.

What the reading says about menu music

  • It is the appetizer: it says the mood of the game before anything is played.
  • The tune has to be caught in the first seconds, so each of these starts on its tune, with no introduction.
  • Nobody knows how long a player stays, so it loops, and the loop must not be heard. One developer made the menu a little faster than the music of play (120 against 100).
  • A short tune that calls and is answered is what people remember (Grant Kirkhope on Banjo-Kazooie). "Sugar rush" is built on that.
  • Bright and cute, measured on title themes: a major key, 126 to 154 beats a minute (Super Mario Party 126, Donkey Kong 140, Kirby 150 and 154), short notes.

Sources: gamedev.net, what goes into a title track · Ninichi, menu against main theme · Composer Code, Grant Kirkhope · Game Developer, Hiroki Kikuta · Hooktheory, Super Mario Party · Donkey Kong · Kirby

How they are made

  • The recipe of the pieces you kept: rich chords under a lead that plays one bar three times, then a cadence.
  • Synth sounds from Surge XT, played from Python (music/menu.py). No sound of a kept piece is used again.
  • Every sound was measured on the beat, with no echo. Every piece is at the game's music level (-20 LUFS).
  • I cannot hear them. They are five guesses built on what you have liked so far, and the choice is yours.

Second round: what the meters found

  • You said every one of the first five sounded bad. I soloed every part and held each mix against the two day pieces you kept (music/mixcheck.py).
  • Several sounds spoke an octave under the key pressed: the lower double and some chords were playing in the bass's register, on top of the bass and the kick.
  • One lead was only a click, one bass buzzed, and no piece had the quiet wide pad both kept pieces stand on. The level was a flat wall, or full of holes.
  • Now: every sound was chosen out of 1,200 measured ones for its closeness to a sound you kept, each part sits in its own register, each piece has its own pad, and held leads are played short.
  • The tunes and chords are the ones of the first round; "Sugar rush" went from 172 to 156 beats a minute.
