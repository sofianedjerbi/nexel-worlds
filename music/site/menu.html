What the reading says about menu music

  • It is the appetizer: it says the mood of the game before anything is played.
  • The tune has to be caught in the first seconds, so each of these starts on its tune, with no introduction.
  • Nobody knows how long a player stays, so it loops, and the loop must not be heard. One developer made the menu a little faster than the music of play (120 against 100).
  • A short tune that calls and is answered is what people remember (Grant Kirkhope on Banjo-Kazooie). Sugar rush, Welcome mat and Jam jar (kept for later) are built on that.
  • Bright and cute, measured on title themes: a major key, 126 to 154 beats a minute (Super Mario Party 126, Donkey Kong 140, Kirby 150 and 154), short notes.

Sources: gamedev.net, what goes into a title track · Ninichi, menu against main theme · Composer Code, Grant Kirkhope · Game Developer, Hiroki Kikuta · Hooktheory, Super Mario Party · Donkey Kong · Kirby

How they are made

  • The recipe of the pieces you kept: rich chords under a lead that plays one bar three times, then a cadence.
  • Synth sounds from Surge XT, played from Python (music/menu.py). No sound of a kept piece is used again. Every sound was chosen by measure, out of 1,700 scanned, for its closeness to a sound you kept.
  • Every sound was measured on the beat, with no echo. Every piece is at the game's music level (-20 LUFS).
  • I cannot hear them. They are guesses built on what you have liked so far, and the choice is yours.
